🧑‍🤝‍🧑 B. Introduce someone

Tell another person about your friend or family member.

This is my friend, Dika.

He is thirteen years old.

He comes from Bandar Lampung.

His hobby is playing football.

His favorite food is fried rice.

Use he / his for a boy or man, and she / her for a girl or woman. Example: “She is my sister. Her hobby is reading.”

Language toolbox

Quick grammar support · Indonesian hints included

Pronouns & possessives

SubjectPossessiveExample
ImyMy hobby is music.
hehisHis hobby is football.
sheherHer hobby is reading.
weourOur class is fun.
theytheirTheir favorite food is noodles.

Subject = siapa yang melakukan / dibicarakan. Possessive = kepunyaan.

Am, is, are & question words

I am 13. · He/She is 13. · We/They are 13.

What = apa · Where = di mana/dari mana · How old = berapa umur

What is your hobby? — My hobby is drawing.

Where are you from? — I'm from Metro.

How old are you? — I'm thirteen.

🎨 Useful vocabulary

Hobbies: drawing, reading, singing, dancing, cooking, cycling, playing football, playing games, gardening.

Food: fried rice, noodles, meatballs, chicken soup, satay, fruit.

Colors: blue, green, purple, red, yellow, black, white.

Subjects: English, Mathematics, Science, Art, Social Studies, PE.

Remember: after “I enjoy,” use an -ing verb: “I enjoy drawing.” You can choose information you feel comfortable sharing.

FACILITATOR VIEW

Suggested 50-minute lesson

  1. Warm-up & topic (5 min)
  2. Explore material and dialogue (10 min)
  3. Introduction Builder (5 min)
  4. Spin and Speak (10 min)
  5. Interview two classmates (10 min)
  6. Introduce a classmate + final challenge (10 min)
  7. If time allows, reflection (5 min)

Adjust timing to class size. Use the projector mode to display prompts for the whole class.

Classroom facilitation

  • Model one short introduction.
  • Pair students and ensure they switch roles.
  • Offer sentence starters, vocabulary hints, and thinking time.
  • Accept different truthful answers.
  • Let students skip private details and use fictional details if preferred.
  • Give specific, kind feedback: “Your answer was clear. Add one detail next time.”

Speaking assessment rubric

Teacher scores the final speaking task. Automated pronunciation scoring is not used.
